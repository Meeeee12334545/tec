“Happy Teachers = Happy Schools.”
[Heading1] 1. Executive Summary
This plan sets out an independent education advisory practice built on a long career of school-based and system-level leadership across curriculum, digital technologies, and now artificial intelligence in education. The business exists to make schools and teachers' jobs easier — not to tell them they are failing, but to help an already-committed profession do its best work with better tools, clearer processes, and less administrative load.
The core proposition is simple: invest in people. By improving teacher capacity and capability, reducing non-teaching workload, and supporting sound curriculum, assessment, technology and AI decision-making, the practice aims to lift teacher retention, staff wellbeing, and student outcomes at the same time — a genuine win-win for schools, staff, students and families.
Engagements begin with a free first meeting to identify what would genuinely benefit a given school, followed by a clear engagement letter setting out services, deliverables and conditions. The guiding rule for the business: only ever offer a service that proactively benefits the client.

[Heading1] 3. Market Opportunity & Rationale
What is the real difference between a school in Ascot and a school in Toowoomba? Largely, socioeconomic placement, and the time and money each school has available to solve the same problems. Well-resourced schools can buy specialist capacity in-house; many others cannot, and their teachers absorb the gap through unpaid time and stretched expertise.
This practice is built on the belief that schools and teachers are already doing their best. The role of the advisor is not to imply otherwise, but to help build on that effort — improving the tools and processes available, at a fraction of the time and cost it would take a school to develop the same capability alone.
“I’m here to make your job easier. Happy Teachers = Happy Schools.”
[Heading2] Cost Context Worth Presenting to Schools
When discussing value with a prospective client, it helps to frame the advisory fee against the real, often invisible costs schools already carry:
[ListParagraph] • The cost of training a new teacher through university
[ListParagraph] • The cost of onboarding a new teacher into a school’s systems and culture
[ListParagraph] • The cost of staff turnover (recruitment, lost institutional knowledge, disruption to students)
[ListParagraph] • The downstream cost implied by staff wellbeing and attrition data
Positioned this way, advisory support is not an added expense — it is a lower-cost, faster way to close a capability gap the school is already paying for in other forms.

[Heading2] Pillar 1 — Curriculum & Assessment Advisory
[ListParagraph] • Whole-school curriculum and assessment plans
[ListParagraph] • Planning and assessment templates for Junior, Secondary and Senior phases
[ListParagraph] • Curriculum mapping — units across subjects
[ListParagraph] • Units of work (e.g. 8-week blocks), assessment tasks and culminating tasks
[ListParagraph] • Process plans: how to write a unit and assessment; how to build task-specific rubrics; how to write report comments; how to plan a timetable, week, term or semester
[ListParagraph] • Understanding the Australian Curriculum — subject-specific guidance, including General Capabilities and Cross-Curriculum Priorities
[ListParagraph] • Understanding standards, elaborations and rubrics, translated into task-specific detail
[Heading2] Pillar 2 — Digital Technologies & AI Advisory
[ListParagraph] • Guidance on shaping a school’s Digital Technologies policy
[ListParagraph] • Guidance on physical staff and student device management
[ListParagraph] • Professional development in digital literacy, file and resource management, and general edtech capability
[ListParagraph] • Advice on how to map and scope Technologies units, including resourcing support
[ListParagraph] • AI-specific advisory: Assessment Policy alignment, Academic Integrity guardrails, AI strategy/policy/direction, an AI Assessment Scale, and an AI Judgement Tool

[ListParagraph] • Support building a school’s AI/Digital Technologies Guidelines document (notional hours, generative AI policy, etc.)
[Heading2] Pillar 3 — Staff Capability, Wellbeing & Sustainability

[Heading1] 6. Target Market
Independent, Catholic and Anglican schools across the socioeconomic spectrum, with particular value for schools that lack in-house curriculum or digital/AI specialist capacity, are navigating staff turnover or wellbeing pressures, or are exposed to emerging AI-related academic integrity risk. Services scale from a single free consultation and small fixed-scope project through to an ongoing advisory retainer.

[Heading1] 9. Measuring & Demonstrating Impact
Statistics are useful for framing the size of a problem, but should be used carefully — to mitigate the gap between data and reality, not to overstate it, and never to misdirect a school’s curriculum trajectory. Relevant data sources to draw on when scoping and reporting on engagements include:
Data Area
What It Shows
NAPLAN / ICT results
Baseline literacy, numeracy and digital capability
Curriculum & subject group development
Trends in subject-level design and delivery
Teacher retention
Staff turnover and succession risk
School improvement measures
Progress against school improvement plans
Staff wellbeing data
Workload, morale and burnout risk
Student / school refusal rates
Engagement and belonging indicators
These feed a simple outcomes framework used to explain impact to a school’s leadership:
Happy Students & Parents
Happy Staff
Skills & Outcomes
Public perception and community confidence
Retention, prestige, and staff wellbeing
Outcomes scaling, academic results, and overall school improvement
The through-line: staff who feel valued and have capacity and wellbeing supported drive both public perception and stronger, gradeable student outcomes — the flywheel the business is designed to turn.
